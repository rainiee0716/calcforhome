# How Many Bags of Concrete in a Yard? The Math Most DIYers Get Wrong

Last spring a neighbor of mine poured a patio slab by hand. He bought 35 bags of 80 lb concrete mix for what he estimated was "about a yard." He ran out with a quarter of the slab still bare, drove back to the store twice, and ended up with a cold joint down the middle of his patio that cracks every winter.

The entire mistake came from one number. A cubic yard of concrete is **45 bags of 80 lb mix** — not 35, not 50. That single conversion decides whether your pour goes smoothly or turns into a two-trip scramble.

## The whole math is one division

A cubic yard is a box three feet on a side: 3 × 3 × 3 = **27 cubic feet**. Every bag of pre-blended concrete prints its yield — the volume of mixed concrete it produces. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.60 cubic feet. So:

**bags per yard = 27 ÷ yield**

- 80 lb bag (0.60 cu ft) → 45 bags
- 60 lb bag (0.45 cu ft) → 60 bags
- 50 lb bag (0.375 cu ft) → 72 bags
- 40 lb bag (0.30 cu ft) → 90 bags

That's it. If a website quotes you a different count for 80 lb bags than 45, it is either rounding from a specific product with a lower yield, or it's wrong.

## What it costs, and where the truck wins

Forty-five bags of 80 lb mix runs **$315–$405** at 2026 home-center prices. A delivered cubic yard of ready-mix runs $150–$200 — roughly half. But trucks charge a short-load fee of $100–$250 below a full load, so the real crossover sits around **1.5 cubic yards**: below it, bags win on price and scheduling; above 2 yards, the truck wins on both.

One more number worth knowing: a yard in bags is **3,600 pounds** no matter which bag size you buy. A half-ton pickup legally carries 1,500–2,000 pounds of payload. Do the trips math before you buy.

## The two mistakes that cause most shortages

**Skipping the waste allowance.** Formwork bows, subgrade settles, and spillage happens. Add 10% on firm ground, 15% if you're forming on dirt or it's your first pour. Ten percent on a full yard is five more bags of 80 lb mix.

**Trusting brand over yield.** Fast-setting and high-strength blends yield a little less — closer to 0.55 cubic feet per 80 lb bag, which moves a full yard from 45 bags toward 50. Check the yield printed on the bag before you multiply.
